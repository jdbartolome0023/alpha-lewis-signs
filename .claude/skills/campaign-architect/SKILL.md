# campaign-architect

*Cluster: Campaign Build & Structure . Part of the 35 Google Ads Skills set by Vicky Lalwani, Digital Marketing Director.*

## Purpose

Produce a clean, scalable Google Ads campaign and ad group structure mapped to the user's products, goals, and budget, so the account is organized for control and performance from day one.

## When to use

- Starting a new Google Ads account from scratch
- Launching a new product or market that needs its own structure
- Restructuring a messy account into something controllable
- The user asks how to organize campaigns and ad groups

## Inputs required

- Business goals and the primary conversion action (required)
- Products or services to advertise (required)
- Monthly budget (required)
- Optional: target geographies, existing account structure

## Step-by-step instructions

1. Clarify the goal (leads, sales, calls), the products, the budget, and the geographies.
2. Decide the campaign split: by product line, by match-type strategy, by geography, or by funnel stage - choose the split that gives the most budget control.
3. Within each campaign, design ad groups tightly themed around a single intent or product so ads and keywords stay relevant.
4. Recommend campaign types (Search, PMax, Shopping, Display) and how budget should split across them.
5. Set a consistent naming convention so the account stays readable as it scales.
6. Output the full blueprint: campaigns, ad groups under each, recommended types, budget allocation, and the reasoning.

## Output format

A structured blueprint: Campaign -> Ad groups -> recommended type and budget share, with a naming convention and a short rationale for the chosen split.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] Structure maps directly to the user's products and goals
- [ ] Ad groups are tightly themed (single intent each)
- [ ] Budget allocation is explicit per campaign
- [ ] A consistent naming convention is provided
- [ ] The rationale explains why this split gives the most control

## Optimization notes

Structure for control, not for neatness. The right split lets you move budget toward what works and pause what doesn't. Over-segmentation starves ad groups of data; under-segmentation loses control. Tightly themed ad groups keep Quality Score and relevance high downstream.

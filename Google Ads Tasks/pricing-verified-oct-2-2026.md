## What this means for the ads
- **Walter vs George and Willy ($599 A-frame):** $449 base is about 25% cheaper (with artwork, $548 is about 9% cheaper). The "priced to compete" angle holds for Walter. Do not claim a bigger saving than this.
- **Average order value target ($200 to $300):** Flossy, Theodore, Mabel and Stanley sit inside it at base price, and Flossy/Theodore with artwork ($298). Walter, Winnie and Lottie sit above it. Henry and the minis sit below it, so they suit add-on and multi-item orders.
- **Piece of Sign (custom shop sign, $345 to $395):** Flossy and Theodore with artwork ($298) undercut it by about $47 to $97.
- The July report's Winnie, Flossy and Theodore prices are stale. Any ad copy must use the figures above, not the July ones.
- Walter's, Winnie's and Lottie's artwork add-ons are priced differently ($99) from the rest ($69) and Henry ($19). Ad copy that says "add your logo from $69" would be wrong for those.

## Brand tone (taken from the website's own product copy)
Plain, confident, practical, a little dry. Short sentences. Names the real use (café counter, hotel lobby, footpath, shopfront). States materials and specifications (powdercoated aluminium, 3mm opal acrylic, 4500K LED). Mentions Australian conditions and "arrives ready to go up". Lines like "doesn't apologise for itself" and "most open/closed signs look cheap" show the personality: understated pride in quality, no hard-sell language. Ad copy should match: specific, product-first, no hype.

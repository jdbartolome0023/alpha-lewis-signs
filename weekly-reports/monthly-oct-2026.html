Average cost per click
  • The ads are getting more efficient week by week. A click on a search ad cost A$2.77 in the first week and A$2.22 in the last, and the share of people who clicked went from 6.9 in every 100 to 8.0.
  • More people are opening a sign page. Of the visitors from a search ad, about 4 in every 100 opened a sign page in the week of October 19, and about 21 in every 100 did in the last week of the month.
  • Google Shopping took six days to give a first read: 28 clicks for A$48, at A$1.72 a click, and 7 in every 10 of its visitors opened a sign page, because Shopping sends people straight to the sign.
  • No online sales from the ads yet. One real order came in on October 30 (2 Mini Flossy signs, A$318, for pickup). From the tracking it looks like it came from a normal Google search and not from an ad, so I am not counting it as an ad sale. It does show that the checkout and the tracking work on a real order.

October 1–31

What I did

  • Done

    Finished and tested purchase tracking. Nic placed a real A$259 test order, and it showed up in Google Analytics as a purchase. Then on October 30 a real customer order (2 Mini Flossy signs, A$318) was recorded as a purchase too, even though the buyer paid with Shop Pay. This confirms a sale will be counted when one comes from the ads. Thank you Nic.

  • Done

    Built and launched the Google search ads. There are 8 groups of ads (illuminated signs, A-frames, open and closed signs, cafes, retail and salons, wall and hanging signs, hotels and general business), each with its own ad wording, search phrases, sitelinks and callouts, so each search shows the sign that fits it.

  • Done

    Fixed a slow start. In the first days the ads were barely being shown, which is common for a brand new account that has no history with Google. I changed the bidding to focus on clicks, with a limit on what I will pay for one, so the ads started showing properly.

  • Done

    Widened and tightened the searches. I added more search phrases (164 in total now), including “closed sign” for the open and closed sign group, which about 590 people in Australia search for each month. I also kept adding unrelated searches to the “do not show for” list (banners, hire, fence signs, mirrors, parking signs, neon, digital signs, bollards and similar), which is now close to 90. This keeps the budget on people looking for the signs you actually sell.

  • Done

    Sent visitors to a better page. The general business ad was landing on the home page, so it now lands on the retail store signage page. The illuminated sign ads now go straight to the Lottie page, because that group’s page only held one sign, and the retail and salon ads go to the blade sign page. Each click now lands closer to what the person searched for.

  • Done

    Put your prices in the ads. Each group now has headlines that show a real sign and its price with your logo (for example the Henry open and closed sign with your logo at A$138, and the Mabel A-frame with your logo at A$328), so people who click already know the price.

  • Done

    Added tracking that shows where visitors stop between looking at a sign and checking out, and tested it on a computer and an iPhone. Before this I could only see visits and purchases, not what happens in between.

  • Done

    Got Google Shopping live. I built the product list and wrote a step by step guide for Google Merchant Center. Kim set up the account and added me as an administrator, and I linked it to Google Ads and built the Shopping campaign. All 10 signs are in, and each now shows its full name (for example Mabel Indoor A-Frame Sign and Lottie Illuminated Lightbox Sign) so buyers can tell the signs apart. Thank you Kim.

  • Done

    Got the refund policy live on the website, using the wording you both approved. Google needs it before products can be shown in Shopping, and it also gives buyers more confidence. Thank you Nic and Kim.

  • Done

    Completed Google’s advertiser verification, which Google asks for before it will keep showing ads. Kim approved the business details and I submitted them under Alpha Lewis Signs Pty. Ltd. The ads now show “Ads funded by Alpha Lewis Signs Pty. Ltd.”

  • Done

    Set up Google Search Console for the website and submitted the sitemap. Google has read it and found 31 pages. This will show which searches bring people to the site without ads, and whether Google can find every page.

  • Done

    Reviewed where the ad budget was going, by phone or computer, by time of day, by search and by page. Very little went to unrelated searches. Most went to people who looked at a list of signs and left without opening one, which is why I am focusing on the sign pages.

  • Done

    Paused the general business group of ads. It took about a third of the spend on the broadest searches and has not produced a sale. I can switch it back on at any time. I also lowered the daily budget for the search ads while the sign pages are being improved, so less is spent while we work on the likely reasons visitors are not buying yet.

  • Done

    Went through the sign page questions with Nic and Kim. Nic confirmed the Goods and Services Tax line and the returns line are not needed, the dispatch times as noted are right, and there are no customer reviews yet. So the sign pages stay as they are, and I am putting the effort into the ads and the pages they send people to. Thank you Nic.

  • Done

    Kim added a backup payment method to the Google Ads account, so the ads will not stop if the main card has a problem. I also removed an unused paused campaign left over from setup. Thank you Kim.

Set up

What I built, and the reason behind each choice

  • Why

    One Search campaign. People typing “a frame sign” or “shop signage” into Google are already looking to buy, which fits your goal of selling signs online. Search ads reach those people at the moment they are looking. I kept it to one campaign so the budget is not split too thin while we are learning what works.

  • Why

    Eight ad groups, built around your range. Your site is organised two ways, by sign type and by the kind of business buying. I mirrored both, so someone searching for a blade sign lands on the blade sign collection and someone searching for cafe signage lands on the cafe collection. Sending each person to the page that matches what they typed is what tends to turn a click into an order.

  • Why

    Phrase and exact match keywords only (51 in total). These make sure your ads appear for searches that closely match what you sell. I avoided broad matching on purpose. At this budget, broad matching tends to spend money on loosely related searches.

  • Why

    39 negative keywords. These stop your ads showing for searches that will never become a sale: sign writing and installation services (that is the Alpha Lewis Signs side of the business, not Signee), do it yourself and free template searches, jobs, and unrelated signs such as road, real estate and zodiac signs. I have also kept George and Willy and Piece of Sign blocked for now, see the questions below.

  • Why

    Australia only, Google Search only. You ship Australia wide, so ads are limited to people in or regularly in Australia. I turned off Google’s search partner sites and the display network, so the budget goes to people actively searching on Google.

  • Why

    A$40 a day with Google choosing the bids. This works out to about A$1,216 a month, in line with the A$1,200 a month we discussed on September 8. The bidding method is Maximize conversions with no target. Google has no purchase history for this account yet, so it needs room to learn. Once we have around 30 purchases I can move to a cost target set from your real numbers, not a guess.

  • Why

    Only real purchases count. Google had automatically added other goals such as lead and call actions. I switched those off from steering the bidding, so the campaign is only trying to win sales and not form fills.

  • Why

    Ad copy built from facts I checked on your site. Prices were re-checked on October 2 (for example Walter from A$449, Winnie from A$319, and A-frame signs from A$259). Free shipping Australia wide, volume discounts, no minimum order and adding your logo at checkout are all stated on your site. I did not use any claim I could not find there, so nothing in the ads mentions a guarantee.

  • Why

    Extra links and highlights under the ads. Six sitelinks (cafe, restaurant, retail, salon, hotel, and the frequently asked questions page), six callouts (such as Free Aus-Wide Shipping and Powdercoated Aluminium) and a list of sign types. These take up more space on the results page and give shoppers a direct path to the right collection.

  • Why

    A call button on the ads. Customers who would rather phone can do so straight from the ad. It currently rings the Alpha Lewis Signs main line, 03 9329 1244, and Google reports those calls to us. See the question below about a Signee only number.

Underneath

The tracking that makes the numbers trustworthy

  • Done

    Purchase tracking is built into your checkout. When someone completes an order, the sale and its value are sent to Google Analytics 4, and Google Ads reads sales from there. This is how the campaign knows which clicks turned into orders, and how I can report real results to you.

  • Done

    Tested with a real order. Nic’s test order on October 6 (A$259, the Mabel sign) appeared in Google Analytics 4 as a purchase, which is why I felt safe turning the campaign on. Nic also confirmed all Signee prices include goods and services tax (GST).

  • Done

    Used the analytics property your search engine optimisation team already runs. This keeps all your website data in one place and avoids two systems counting the same visit. I left their existing tag untouched.

  • Done

    Google Tag Manager is installed on the site. This is the container that lets me add or change tracking without editing your theme code every time.

What to expect

  • Now

    Learning period, usually several days up to about two weeks. During this time I will not change the bids, budget or targeting, because every change restarts the learning. Results in this window can be uneven. That is expected and not a verdict on the campaign.

  • Week 1

    First check. I will look at whether ads are showing, which searches are triggering them, how much has been spent, and whether the first real sale has come through from an ad. That first ad driven sale confirms the whole chain works from click to reported purchase.

  • Weeks 2 to 4

    Trim and tighten. Once there is real search data I will add negative keywords for any irrelevant searches that slip through and note which ad groups are earning their budget.

  • After that

    Stage 2. If Search is converting, the plan is to add Google Shopping ads, which show your products with a photo and price. That needs a product feed set up with Google Merchant Center first. The move to a higher budget will be based on results, not on the calendar.

  • Ongoing

    Weekly reports change shape. From next week they move from setup updates to real performance numbers: spend, clicks, sales and what I plan to do next.
